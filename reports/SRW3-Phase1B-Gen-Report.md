## 3. CM2 formalization: physical keys vs logical resources (Part II)

CM2 is the observation that physical storage identity and logical resource identity can come apart: two distinct storage keys can resolve to the same logical resource, and a system that reasons only about physical key footprints will under-approximate the logical effects of a write through an alias. The generalized semantics makes this distinction explicit with three additions to the syntax: a finite sort `ResourceId`, an external resolution table `resmap : StKey → ResourceId` (the model input `Resolves`), and the requirement that the abstract counterexample be representable: `(appA, slot7)` and `(appB, slot3)` are distinct `StKey`s while `Resolves(K1) = Resolves(K2) = resP`.

The counterexample was constructed **before** any defense, as the mandate requires. Program `cm2_counterexample.srw3`: A initializes `stK(appA,7) ← 5`; B writes `stK(appB,3) ← 6` through the alias; the logical resource invariant `IRP` requires `stK(appA,7) + stK(appB,3) ≤ 10`. Under the naive registry (`aliasNaive`), the resource obligation `IRP` is app-anchored at the primary holder only, so B's aliased write raises **no** obligation: the gate commits, and the committed state violates `IRP` (5 + 6 = 11 > 10). The purely-physical footprint system is shown failing on its own terms — the true logical effect set is `{resP}`, the declared physical footprint is `{stK(appB,3)}`, and the missed alias obligation is exactly the gap between them. This demo is a permanent regression: `test_cm2_counterexample_naive_commits_alias_unsafe` in the Python oracle asserts both the commit decision **and** the violated invariant on the committed state.

## 4. Alias-aware effect semantics (Part III)

The defense was derived from the counterexample rather than chosen from a catalog. The effect model normalizes physical footprints to logical resources by the closure `AliasClosure(E) = { r | ∃k∈E. Resolves(k) = r }`, realized as the total function `logicalOf(F, resmap)`; obligation applicability is then evaluated per scope: app-scope edges fire on the app projection `appsOf(F)` (Phase-0 behavior, unchanged), **resource-scope edges `edgeR(J, Rs)` fire on `logicalOf(F, resmap)`**, and authority-scope edges (Part V/VI) fire on `authOf(F)`. Completeness keeps its Phase-0 form at the physical granularity (`wfCheck` enforces `FT ⊆ FD` under `enforce`); the logical-level statement follows by monotonicity — under the physical premise, `AliasClosure(FT) ⊆ AliasClosure(FD)`, so applicability computed from the declared footprint coincides with the true one. The strengthened completeness condition under which CM2-style mis-decisions become impossible is therefore: *every resource obligation is covered by an `edgeR` whose scope contains its resource dependencies (`resourceComplete`), and applicability is evaluated over the alias closure of the declared footprint*.

Two deliberate non-choices are recorded. First, the alias relation itself (`resmap`) is an **external model input** — the calculus consumes the resolution table as data and enforces obligations uniformly over its closure, but does not derive which keys alias which resources; this is assumption A5, classified ASSUMED and stated in the header of `srw3gen.k` (a wrong or incomplete `resmap` is a modeling error outside the calculus). Second, no axiom asserts "the alias relation is sound"; the theorems below are conditional on the closure being the model's declared input, which keeps the proof surface honest.

## 5. CM3 formalization: membership is not aggregate safety (Part V)

CM3 is the observation that authorization-closure membership checks compose badly under delegation: individual grants can each be within their own bound while their sum exceeds the root budget. The generalized semantics adds a minimal bounded-delegation representation — `cap : AppId → Int` (granted capacity), `parent : AppId → AppId` (delegation tree), and consumed usage `usage-c/usage-p` threaded through the commitment boundary — with the aggregate predicate stated as an evaluated obligation `IAGG: usage(appC) + usage(appD) ≤ cap(appB)`, over the individual-capacity obligations `ICAPC/ICAPD`. The representation is deliberately minimal: nothing in the semantics hard-codes a governance model; the topology (both delegates under root `appB`) is the concrete instance used by the theorem instances and demos.

The counterexample again preceded the defense. Program `cm3_overflow_counterexample.srw3`: root grants budget 10; delegates are granted 6 and 6; the first transition consumes 6 (within every individual bound, gate commits), the second consumes 6 (each membership check `ICAPC/ICAPD` evaluated in isolation passes). Under the naive registry (`authNaive`), the gate commits the second transition and the committed usage state violates the aggregate budget: 6 + 6 = 12 > 10. This establishes by executable witness that `AuthorizationMembership ≠ AggregateAuthoritySafety` — the permanent regression asserts both the naive commit and the violated aggregate invariant in the Python oracle (`test_cm3_overflow_counterexample_naive_commits`, `test_cm3_membership_is_not_aggregate_safety`).

## 6. The authority-aware commit gate (Part VI)

The scientific test of Part VI is whether CM3 can be absorbed as **another obligation class** without creating a second gate mechanism. It can, and the implementation is exactly four evaluated additions inside the existing machinery: the aggregate invariant `IAGG` is an ordinary evaluated equation over the prospective usage bundle; the authority-scope edge `edgeA(IAGG, {appC, appD})` makes it applicable whenever the declared footprint's authority projection touches either delegate (consumption declares an `AppId` item into the footprint, in the same discipline as writes); the obligation universe gains the three authority obligations; and the gate's accept rule continues to require **one** conjunction `allHoldG(applicableGen(registry, FD, resmap), P, UP, CP)` over the prospective bundle. The accept rule remains the only transition that updates `<committed>/<usage-c>`; rejection reasons remain uniform (`reject` on violated obligations, `reject-auth` on failed membership, `blocked-effect-incomplete` on failed `wfCheck`); no "authority gate" branch exists. The K claim `GATE3-AGREE` verifies the architecture claim mechanically: the certificate-predicted accept condition equals the real gate's evaluated applicable-obligation conjunction, with the aggregate obligation evaluated from `cap` — no special branch.

## 7. The generalized ghost theorems (Parts IV and VII, and the full suite)

The ∀n theorems are mechanized over ghost-instrumented copies of `srw3gen.k`, built by the same additive discipline as Phase 0-D (semantics bytes identical except `//GHOST:`-marked lines; fidelity certified mechanically in `ghost_manifest_gen.json`). Each artifact carries an observer pair (`ghostSafe*`, `ghostMatches*`), a certificate transformer applied at the commitment point (`ghostApply*` on accept, `ghostUndo*` on reject), and the claim suite: BASE/A/R (obligations and preservation), SOUND-I/F (certificate→real-state bridge), GATE-AGREE (architecture claim), ISO/T1 (non-circular diagnostics), T2 (the ∀n theorem, `[circularity]`), M (the stronger GhostMatches-at-every-termination form), and NEG (the non-vacuity control, which must fail).

The results, per suite, are: **cm2_alias** (alias-aware model, class `tK2`): 11 PROVED + GH2-NEG fails as designed — including GH2-T2/GH2-M. **cm3_auth** (aggregate-authority model, classes `tC`/`tD`): 11 PROVED + GH3-NEG fails as designed — including **GH3-T2/GH3-M**, the CM3 aggregate-authority lineage-length safety theorem. **gen_full** (single generalized model — multi-write across four accounts, aliased resources, and authority consumption in one calculus, class `tG`): 11 PROVED + GH4-NEG fails as designed — including **GH4-T2/GH4-M**, the generalized ∀n theorem. Each T2 instance is stated in the mandated form: `InitialSafe_*(s0) ∧ SecurityClosed_*(registry, enforce, …) → ∀n. Safe_*(s_n)`, with the closure premise evaluated against the concrete registry term (non-vacuous by construction, and NEG demonstrates the premise is load-bearing).

Two proof-engineering defects were found and fixed during this stage, both recorded because they are exactly the failure modes a reviewer must look for in this encoding. (1) **Variable correlation**: the circular claims shared the variable `N1` between the transition amount `tC(N1)`/`tG(V1,V2,N1)` and the lineage counter cell `<lineageNext> N1`. The shared variable silently correlates "the amount being consumed in this cycle" with "the lineage index", the circular re-application stalls, and the proof diverges (GH3-T2 exceeded a 2400 s budget; a depth-bounded run exposed the residual `#Not(N = N1)`). The cm2 suite — which separates the two (amount `V1` vs counter `N1`) — closed, confirming the diagnosis. Fix: claims-module-only rename of the amount variable (`NA/NA1/NA2`), theorem statements unchanged (the amount was already implicitly quantified independently); regenerated artifacts, manifest, and verdicts. (2) **Missing concrete-input rules**: the gen_full ghost module referenced the concrete model inputs `rmPQ()/capG()/parentG()` without defining them, so every transition-touching GH4 claim — including the pure-evaluation `GATE4-AGREE` — stuck at exit 113. Fix: add the three syntax declarations and defining rules (values identical to the cm2/cm3 instances and the demo runner), rekompile, re-run. Both fixes are visible as dated comments in the fragments and in the v2 header of `ghost_gen_theorem.txt` (v1 transcript preserved as `ghost_gen_theorem.v1-sharedvar.txt`).

## 9. KEVM generalized binding (additive extension)

The frozen Phase-1A binding (`k/kevm/srw3-kevm.k`) is **not modified**. The extension module `k/kevm/srw3-gen-binding.k` (`SRW3-GEN-EVM`, imports the binding) adds the two Phase-1B obligation classes over REAL prospective EVM storage — `#w3IAliasG`: the two delegate accounts' slot-3 storages mirror one logical resource and must agree (the EVM shape of CM2: distinct physical keys, shared logical identity); `#w3IAggG`: the delegates' slot-2 consumption sum must stay within the root account's slot-2 budget (the EVM shape of CM3) — and defines `#w3GateG`, which keeps the base gate's exact shape: one evaluated conjunction (`#w3OblG` = IOL ∧ ILD ∧ IOLD ∧ IAliasG ∧ IAggG) over real storage, same threaded state carrier, same commit/restore machinery. Slots 0/1 continue to carry the Phase-0 price/liquidation data so both obligation generations compose without interfering.

Two demos exercise the extension end-to-end, with verdicts read from the threaded carrier (matrix-v3 convention; krun exit 1 is the designed terminal state of the carrier): `evm_gen_positive` (b1=30, b2=40, budget 100, alias pair 7 == 7) → **COMMIT** (n=1, h=0, full storage snapshot committed, lineage appended); `evm_gen_overflow` (60 + 50 > 100) → **REJECT + RESTORE** (n=0, h=-1, all committed storages restored). Both demos run under the haskell and llvm backends with **identical** carriers (`transcripts/audit/evm_gen_equivalence.txt`). This demonstrates the Part VI claim at the real-execution layer: CM2/CM3 enter KEVM purely as additional evaluated obligations of the unchanged one-gate architecture.

## 13. Assumptions (cumulative, classified)

Inherited from Phase 0-D: A1 (dependency oracles are auditable manual derivations), A2 (opaque commitments at the abstract layer), A3 (authorization evidence = membership + evaluated obligations; no signatures), A4 (closure premises evaluated against concrete registry terms). ## 14. Limitations and what remains open

The generalized theorems are proven over the abstract ghost-instrumented semantics with the concrete bounded universe used across the project (four accounts, two resources, three authority obligations); the universes are explicit parameters in `securityClosedGenU`, but the mechanized instances are the concrete ones. The alias table and delegation topology are inputs (A5/A6), so the theorems are conditional on their correctness — deriving `resmap` from EVM storage-layout facts, and capacity trees from on-chain grant logs, remain future binding work. Tier-3 universal reconciliation and the cryptographic lineage binding are untouched and retain their Phase-1A classifications. The KEVM generalized demos exercise storage-level obligations; gas-sensitive and reentrancy-shaped obligation classes are not modeled. Finally, the sandbox reset destroyed the phase-1A git objects; Phase-1B provenance is carried by the fidelity manifests, sha-verified recovery, and a fresh self-contained bundle rather than by continuity of git history (§2).
